# 05. UX Patterns and Micro-interactions

## Why UX matters

Animations are most useful when they reinforce the user experience. A motion should guide the eye, answer a question, or confirm an action.

## Common UX patterns

### 1. Press feedback
When a button is tapped, show a small scale or ripple effect.

### 2. Loading feedback
Use skeletons, spinners, or Lottie loaders while data loads.

### 3. Success confirmation
After an action like save or purchase, show a checkmark or completion animation.

### 4. Transition feedback
Animate screen entry and exit to make navigation feel fluid.

### 5. Scroll-based motion
Use parallax and reveal motion to make feeds and dashboards feel premium.

## Recommended interaction timing

- 150–250ms: quick button press feedback
- 250–500ms: screen or card reveal
- 500–700ms: onboarding or major transition

## Good UX principle

The best animations are intuitive, subtle, and consistent. They should never distract from the task.

## Design checklist

- Is the animation meaningful?
- Does it improve clarity?
- Does it keep performance smooth?
- Does it respect accessibility settings?
- Does it feel consistent across the app?

## Example micro-interaction ideas

- card lift on hover/click
- bottom sheet slide-up
- Snackbar slide-in and auto-dismiss
- filter chips expand and collapse smoothly
- toolbar collapse on scroll
- onboarding slides with gentle motion

## Final recommendation

Treat animation as UI communication, not decoration. If it helps users understand the app, it is valuable.
